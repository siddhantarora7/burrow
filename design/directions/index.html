Three ways burrow could look and feel.

Each direction takes a different slice of your list (imperfect, squishy, japandi, retro, pixel, glass) and commits to it, so the result doesn't turn to mush. All three share the same pet rig: three species (mochi, tofu, onigiri), six states, with personality from posture and timing rather than detail.

Everything here is live code, not images. Use the toggles above to switch the light/dark theme or show a single species. Pet names and events are demo data.

A

sticker book lead direction

Japanese stationery: grid notebook paper, die-cut vinyl stickers, washi tape and hanko stamps. Pets are stickers that come alive. The burrow is a doodle in a shared notebook, and your friends' pets are stuck into its rooms. Imperfect comes from riso misregistration and a hand-drawn line that boils, and the color lives only in the stickers.

what it took from the declined directions

from the op-art textile: the burrow is one seamless field. Tunnels redraw to any width and grow as friends join, never a grid of tiles.
from ebru marbling: the day leaves marks. Every finished task leaves a hanko stamp or crumb that stays until midnight, so one glance shows how today went.
from teletext: everything snaps to the 5mm notebook grid, and every burrow gets a short address you can type.
Signature moment

When a friend's agent ships, a red hanko stamp thunks onto their room and every other sticker in the burrow hops once.

Reaches across surfaces

Share cards are literally stickers: a die-cut pet on a white border with a stats strip. The CLI prints a tiny ascii sticker on hatch.

Honest risk

It could slide into kawaii merch if the color isn't held to four inks. The boiling line needs pre-baked path variants (no live SVG filters) to hold 60fps with 10+ pets.

B

glass burrow my pick

Japandi earth seen through glass, like an ant farm on a desk. The page is a cross-section: a strip of real sky that follows your local time, and soil layers below with carved rooms, one per friend. Pets are matte, squishy and colored, with no outlines. Glass appears only where it would in that world: the pane over the soil, and frosted tags and controls.

why I'd pick it

It's the name, made literal. "Join my burrow" and the picture in your head match the page you land on.
The sky is a free glance signal: at 2am the whole burrow is dark, with lamps lit in the rooms of friends who are still working.
It's the easiest to scale to 10+ pets: rooms are generated, so a bigger group just means a deeper burrow.
Signature moment

A treat drops from the surface, rolls down the tunnel and lands in a friend's room. Cheers travel the same way, as a little light.

Reaches across surfaces

The landing page scrolls downward into the earth: each section is a deeper soil layer, and the privacy section sits at bedrock.

Honest risk

It's the most expected reading of the name, and it's illustration-heavy. Soil, rooms and sky have to be procedural SVG to stay crisp and cheap, and the glass must stay subtle or it looks like a 2021 Dribbble shot.

C

pocket alternate

A handheld from 1998, redrawn sleek. The burrow lives on a four-shade LCD under real glass: pets are 32px sprites with two- or three-frame motion, and the surrounding site is clean and modern. Status reads from across the room because every sprite is a silhouette first.

verdict

Competitive. It wins on glanceability and on developers recognizing it, and loses on warmth and on "color in pets", since four greens can't carry individual pet colors.
If you want pixel art without the whole world, A or B can ship a pixel "pocket mode" for the menubar or terminal later.
Signature moment

When your agent needs you, the screen blinks twice and the LED on the shell turns red: the only red anywhere.

Reaches across surfaces

The terminal gets the same sprites as half-block ANSI art, and share cards look like screenshots of the device.

Honest risk

Pixel retro is a crowded lane for dev toys and pulls toward Tamagotchi nostalgia, which /buddy and Tamamon already own.

Also considered

These were weighed and set aside. Each one still donated a discipline to A, and any of them can come back if you ask.

op-art textiledeclined

A black-and-white wave field. Too cold for pets, but its seamless repeat became A's tunnels that never show a seam.

ebru marblingdeclined

Floating pigment with irreversible strokes. Too abstract to read at a glance, but it gave A "the day leaves marks".

teletext pagesdeclined

A 40×24 cell grid on black. Too harsh for cozy, but it gave A the strict grid and typeable burrow addresses.

Not shown: a provenance ribbon and a curved paper fold (they stay in the pool for a re-roll). There's also the standard look for this category, always available if you want it: a sunny cozy-game meadow with pets wandering on grass.
